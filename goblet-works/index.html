At the bench

Glass only moves while it glows. Keep it hot and turning, and keep going back to the glory hole. A goblet is three pieces of glass: a bowl you blow, a stem you pull, and a foot you paddle flat.

Making a goblet

    Glass colour

    Applies to the next gather or bit, so you can make a coloured foot or stem.

    Pace

    Studio cools glass about twice as fast, more like a real shop.

    How to make a goblet

    1. Gather G: dip the pipe into the furnace. Gather a second time for a bigger piece.
    2. Blow: hold B. Hot, thin walls inflate fastest, so warm the glass evenly before you blow.
    3. Reheat: hold R when the glow fades. Glass you work below about 550 °C cracks.
    4. Jacks J: hold them on the glass to squeeze it down to the pointer's radius. Pinch a jack line near the pipe; that's where the piece will crack off later.
    5. Stem: press N to add a hot bit to the end. Grab it with Tweezers P and drag away from the pipe to pull a stem.
    6. Foot: add another bit, then push the Paddle F against the end to flatten it into a disc.
    7. Transfer X: a punty goes on the foot, and the pipe cracks off at the jack line.
    8. Open O: drag outward inside the rim to flare it. Use Shears S to trim a ragged edge.
    9. Anneal A while the piece is still above 400 °C.

    The pipe keeps rolling unless you stop it with T. Hot glass on a still pipe sags; Block K and turning bring it back to centre. Ctrl/⌘ Z undoes, even a crack.

    The physics is simplified so you can build intuition. It won't predict a real piece exactly.
